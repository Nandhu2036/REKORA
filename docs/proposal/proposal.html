01 / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Engineering projects should not end at submission.

Today's student engineering projects involve a highly disconnected set of activities. A typical engineering student must independently navigate project discovery, component sourcing, CAD modification, fabrication, coding, testing, and documentation. Once a project is evaluated, the physical hardware is often discarded, and the engineering knowledge developed during the process is lost.

REKORA connects these fragmented activities into one continuous ecosystem.

02 / THE PROBLEM

The academic engineering lifecycle is economically inefficient and structurally fragmented.

01
Fragmented Resources

Ideation, component sourcing, CAD modification, manufacturing, and documentation exist in isolated silos, causing immense friction for students.

02
Repeated Hardware Purchases

Students frequently purchase expensive components and hardware kits that are only required temporarily for a single prototype.

03
Limited Access to Fabrication

Moving from digital CAD to a physical prototype is a bottleneck. Students struggle with material selection and accessing 3D printing facilities.

04
Loss of Project Knowledge

Once a project is evaluated, the insights, troubleshooting steps, and design logic disappear. There is no centralized system to capture output.

05
Projects Restart From Zero

Because prior work is inaccessible, students cannot easily build upon previous engineering solutions.

06
Limited Continuity

Subsequent cohorts are forced to build identical basic systems year after year, preventing the organic evolution of ambitious engineering.

03 / THE OPPORTUNITY

The current inefficiency represents a substantial opportunity to redefine academic engineering.

By consolidating the fragmented components of project development into a unified platform, we can reduce the financial barrier to innovation while dramatically improving the quality of student output.

There is an unprecedented opportunity to shift the paradigm from "disposable student projects" to "cumulative engineering assets." A platform that captures this value will not only serve students but will become an indispensable infrastructure layer for modern technical institutions.

The integration of project discovery, hardware sourcing, and digital repositories opens the door for a circular academic economy—where resources are rented, reused, and continuously iterated upon.

05 / HOW REKORA WORKS

Discovery & AI

Users begin with a problem, budget, or components. The platform algorithmically recommends suitable initiatives, while the AI Project Engineer acts as a technical consultant to frame the constraints and generate a blueprint.

Component Sourcing

To reduce unnecessary purchases, REKORA integrates project BOMs directly with inventory availability. Students can choose to Buy, Rent, Use Existing, or Borrow components on an itemized basis.

CAD & Fabrication

REKORA connects digital design to the physical build. Students customize CAD models directly aligned with their requirements, then route them to campus 3D printing and manufacturing workflows.

Build & Documentation

During physical assembly, REKORA provides integration logic, troubleshooting, and standardized documentation frameworks to support academic submission rigor.

Project DNA

Every completed project receives a persistent digital identity preserving the exact configuration of hardware, software, and logic for future reference.

Evolution & Marketplace

Future users fork the repository to improve the physical design or code, while senior cohorts trade unused hardware via a peer-to-peer exchange.

09 / PROJECT DNA & VERSION EVOLUTION

Every completed project can receive a persistent digital identity known as its Project DNA. This preserves the project objective, architecture, BOM, source code, CAD, testing results, and lessons learned.

PROJECT: Autonomous Waste Collector
│
├── v1.0 (Initial Prototype)

├── v1.1

│

This version control philosophy allows future students to study, build, modify, test, and contribute to an existing project rather than discarding it. The project becomes a continuously evolving engineering asset.

17 / RISK, RESPONSIBLE USE & ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Risk & Mitigation Strategy

Risk Factor Mitigation Approach
Low component margins Focus platform revenue heavily on integrated kits, high-margin rentals, and fabrication services rather than commodity trading.
Inventory capital lock-in Start exclusively with highly standardized, high-utilization components in a strictly controlled inventory environment.
Hardware damage during rental Implement QR lifecycle tracking, condition-check protocols, security deposits, and mandatory physical inspection upon return.
High technical support load Leverage the AI Project Engineer for baseline troubleshooting, supplemented by comprehensive documentation and tiered support structures.
Intellectual Property issues Establish clear project ownership terms, contributor attribution, and open-academic licensing records within the version history.
Operational complexity Restrict the initial rollout entirely to a controlled college pilot environment to standardize workflows before any expansion.

Academic Integrity Boundary

REKORA DOES NOT SELL READY-MADE PROJECT SUBMISSIONS.
The platform strictly provides engineering resources, prototyping support, technical guidance, hardware access, and documentation frameworks. The student's actual implementation, testing, cognitive understanding, and academic responsibility remain entirely their own.
